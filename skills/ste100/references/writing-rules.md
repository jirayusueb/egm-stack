# STE100 writing rules, paraphrased

Source: ASD-STE100 Simplified Technical English, https://www.asd-ste100.org, Issue 9 (January 2025).

This file is an original paraphrase of publicly documented constraints. It is not the standard text. The standard and its dictionary are copyright ASD and are not reproduced here. The rule families follow the order of the official rule set, not its wording, and the numbering below is not the official rule number. Cite the official rule when you report a finding.

This is an aide, not the standard. Obtain the free official copy for conformance.

## Approved words and parts of speech

- Use an approved word from the dictionary, or a technical noun or technical verb that names a thing or an action in the reader's subject field.
- An approved word has one meaning and one part of speech. Do not use it as another part.
- Use the approved spelling and the approved form. Do not use a regional variant.
- Do not use a synonym for an approved word. The dictionary names the approved alternative.
- A technical noun or technical verb must name a real thing or action in the field. It is not a cover for a general word that the dictionary rejects.
- Give each technical noun and technical verb one meaning. Put it in a glossary when the document uses more than a few.
- Do not use a general word in a technical sense unless the dictionary approves that sense.

## Multi-word nouns

- Keep a multi-word noun to three words or fewer.
- Count the words that modify the head noun. A hyphenated word counts as one word.
- Break a longer string with a preposition. Write "the panel on the fuel pump", not "the fuel pump panel".
- Put the modifier next to the word it modifies.
- Use the same multi-word noun for the same thing everywhere.

## Permitted verb forms

- A sentence uses one of these verb forms. The infinitive, the imperative, the simple present, the simple past, the simple future, or the past participle as an adjective.
- Do not use the present perfect, the past perfect, or the future perfect.
- Do not use the continuous form for an action.
- Do not use the conditional for a fact. Use the present or the future form.
- Do not use "shall". Write the imperative or "must".
- Keep one tense in one paragraph unless a time change forces another.
- In a procedure, write the step as an imperative. "Remove the cover", not "the cover must be removed".

## No auxiliary complex constructions

- Do not build a verb from more than one auxiliary.
- Use only the modal verbs that the dictionary approves. Do not stack a modal on another verb form.
- Do not write "be going to", "have to", or "be able to" where a simple approved form says the same thing.
- Avoid "there is" and "there are" when a direct sentence works.
- Do not use "being" as a verb.

## -ing forms

- Use an -ing word only as a technical noun, such as "landing gear", or as a modifier in a technical noun.
- Do not use -ing for an action that is in progress. Write a finite verb.
- Do not start a sentence with an -ing clause.
- Do not join two instructions with an -ing clause.

## Active voice

- Write in the active voice and name the actor.
- In a procedure, the actor is the reader. Write the imperative.
- Use the passive only in a description, and only when the actor is unknown or does not matter.
- Do not hide the actor to make a sentence shorter.

## Sentence length

- A procedure step has 20 words or fewer.
- A descriptive sentence has 25 words or fewer.
- Count every word, including articles and numbers.
- A note or a warning follows the descriptive limit.
- A table entry that is a sentence follows the same limit.
- Split a long sentence at a natural boundary. Do not drop information to fit the limit.

## One instruction per sentence

- Put one action in one sentence.
- Do not join two actions with "and" unless they happen together as one action.
- A condition and its action can share a sentence when the condition comes first.
- Do not put a second instruction in a parenthetical phrase.
- Do not put a second instruction in a note or a warning.

## No omitted sentence parts

- Keep the subject, the verb, and the object. Do not drop the subject to sound brisk.
- Keep the articles. Write "Remove the cover", not "Remove cover".
- Keep "that" when it marks the clause.
- Do not use an arrow, a slash, or a symbol in place of words.
- Write a sentence, not a fragment.
- Do not drop a repeated verb in a parallel sentence.

## One topic per paragraph

- Give each paragraph one topic.
- Keep a paragraph to six sentences or fewer.
- Start the paragraph with a sentence that states the topic.
- Do not mix a procedure and a description in one paragraph.
- Do not put a list where a sentence would read faster.

## Vertical lists

- Use a vertical list for a set of items or a set of steps.
- Put one item on each line.
- Start each item with the same part of speech.
- Introduce the list with a complete sentence.
- Number the items in a sequence. Use a bullet for a plain set.
- Make each item a phrase or a sentence, not one of each.
- Keep the list to one level unless the task needs a second level.

## Safety instructions

- Start a safety instruction with a clear command or a clear condition.
- Name the hazard and the result.
- Put the warning before the step that it guards.
- Use "WARNING" for a risk of injury or death. Use "CAUTION" for a risk of damage.
- Do not put the condition after the command.
- Give the action as an imperative.

## Consistent terminology

- Give one name to one thing and use that name everywhere.
- Do not cycle synonyms for the same thing.
- Do not rename a thing that did not change.
- Keep the same spelling and the same part of speech for a term.
- Keep a glossary for the technical nouns and technical verbs.
- Use the dictionary alternative when the dictionary rejects a word.
