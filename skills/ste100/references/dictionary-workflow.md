# STE100 dictionary workflow

The dictionary is the other half of the standard. The rules alone cannot certify a word. Read this file before you check any word.

## Get the copy

1. Register on https://www.asd-ste100.org.
2. Download the official ASD-STE100 Issue 9 document. It is free and it includes the dictionary.
3. Keep the copy outside the repo, or add its path to the gitignore. Never vendor the file, an extract, or a screenshot of it.
4. Never copy a rule, a definition, an example, or an entry into a deliverable.

## Read an entry

A dictionary entry gives the approved word, its part of speech, its approved meaning, the alternatives that the standard permits, and example sentences. An approved word is printed in uppercase. A rejected word is printed in lowercase and points to the approved alternative. A word that is absent may still be a technical noun or technical verb for the subject field.

## Check a word

1. Find the word in the copy.
2. If the copy approves it with the part of speech you need, use it.
3. If the copy rejects it, use the alternative that the entry names.
4. If the word is absent, decide whether it is a technical noun or technical verb for the field. Add it to the document glossary and use one meaning.
5. Never guess an approval. When the copy is missing or a page is unreadable, mark the word unchecked and stop the word-level claim.

## Report

- Cite the issue you used, as in "Checked against the ASD-STE100 Issue 9 dictionary".
- List every word you could not check.
- Say that the check is not a conformance claim. A conformance claim needs a full manual check against the official document.

## Maintenance

Issue 9 is the current issue. When a new issue ships, replace the copy and record the new issue number in the report. Do not mix entries from two issues in one report.
